# Candidate research contribution

## Core hypothesis

A dynamic, provenance-aware trust layer can improve **calibrated reliance** on healthcare AI: people should rely on AI when evidence and safety signals support it and appropriately question or reject it when they do not.

## Candidate contribution

TRUST-MED combines three mechanisms in one auditable loop:

1. **Multi-agent verification** — specialized agents independently inspect context, evidence, safety and governance.
2. **Knowledge fabric** — claims, evidence, predictions, decisions and outcomes are connected with provenance and temporal validity.
3. **Dynamic trust** — a multidimensional trust profile converts evidence, reliability, uncertainty, agreement, patient fit, safety and lifecycle signals into an explicit reliance policy.

The novelty claim should remain a research hypothesis until a formal literature review and empirical comparison establish what is and is not already known.

## First experiment

Compare:

- Control: AI recommendation shown without TRUST-MED.
- Intervention: same recommendation plus TRUST-MED evidence, uncertainty, disagreement and governance signals.

Primary outcomes:

- clinical decision quality in a synthetic/benchmark task;
- appropriate reliance and error detection;
- trust calibration;
- time to decision;
- override behavior;
- understanding of evidence and uncertainty.
